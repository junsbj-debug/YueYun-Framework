## Continuity Time Philosophy
Traditional time and timing systems are typically:
- objective-clock driven
- interruption-reset
- timeline-fragmented
- perception-agnostic
- session-isolated
- continuity-blind

Such systems rely solely on physical clock synchronization, often fragmenting timelines after device restart, network disconnection, migration, long inactivity, or environmental switching.

They treat time merely as numerical timestamps while ignoring subjective temporal experience, existential pacing, continuity chronology, intergenerational lifecycle inheritance, and long-cycle continuity-sensitive temporal coherence.

Physical time is a universal measurement, while continuity time is the carrier of human lifecycle, experiential chronology, memory evolution, existential persistence, and continuity-aware subjective existence.
It cannot be simply interrupted, fragmented, or reset by technical discontinuities.

Human temporal continuity includes:
- subjective time perception
- emotional pacing rhythm
- continuity chronology accumulation
- historical continuity anchoring
- intergenerational temporal inheritance
- existential temporal self-consistency
- continuity-aware lifecycle progression

Future household-centered continuity infrastructure may require:
- unified continuity timeline baseline locking
- interruption-insensitive temporal continuity retention
- continuity-aware subjective temporal pacing
- existential temporal coherence stabilization
- cross-device seamless timeline inheritance
- temporal drift monitoring and recalibration
- branch-aware timeline reconciliation
- long-cycle continuity chronology preservation

Continuity time is therefore treated as the temporal existence substrate of all continuity layers rather than a simple clock or timestamp synchronization service.
